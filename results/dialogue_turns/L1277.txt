KE_{\text{initial}} = \frac{1}{2} \times 40 \, \text{kg} \times (0.60 \, \text{m/s})^2 = 7.2 \, \text{J}
\]
\[
KE_{\text{final}} = \frac{1}{2} \times 40 \, \text{kg} \times (0.20 \, \text{m/s})^2 = 0.8 \, \text{J}
\]
\[
\Delta KE = KE_{\text{final}} - KE_{\text{initial}} = 0.8 \, \text{J} - 7.2 \, \text{J} = -6.4 \, \text{J}
\]

### Step 3: Use the Work-Energy Principle
The work done by friction is calculated as:
\[
W_{\text{friction}} = \Delta KE - W_{\text{applied}} = -6.4 \, \text{J} - 20 \, \text{J} = -26.4 \, \text{J}
\]

The negative sign indicates that friction is doing negative work, dissipating energy as heat. Therefore, the increase in thermal energy is the magnitude of this work:
\[
\Delta E_{\text{th}} = |W_{\text{friction}}| = 26.4 \, \text{J}
\]

\[
\boxed{26.4}
